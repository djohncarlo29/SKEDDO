---
name: App header scroll-edge behavior
description: Shared pinned header glass edge and large-title movement across app pages.
---

## Rule
All second-level titles and ordinary scroll content must pass beneath the same first-level `LiquidGlassScrollEdge`; do not substitute title translucency or add per-screen/per-layer blur. In Calendar Month and Year views, the title row and its navigation controls are actual first children of each calendar scroll view, not AppShell header content moved by notifications. AppShell keeps only the first-level toolbar fixed. Other large titles may continue to track their owning scrollable's actual position without a fixed positive maximum. Floor negative elastic overscroll at zero, clamp glass-edge progress to the title-slot length, and keep offsets scoped to the page/view whose scroll controller owns them. Calendar Day timeline scrolling does not own the app header; exclude its notifications. Preserve content-matched landscape surfaces.

**Why:** Transparent backplates alone do not guarantee compositing: a title clip or a scroll viewport's hard clip can keep that layer from ever reaching the glass edge. The user explicitly requires titles and content to share one first-level boundary after seeing inconsistent behavior across tabs and Settings. Clamping the title itself leaves it stuck beneath the pinned toolbar instead of allowing it to scroll away; summing gesture deltas can diverge from ballistic or restored positions. Flutter's bouncing physics can report pixels below the minimum extent, which otherwise translates the title back down behind the toolbar. A global notification listener also sees Day timeline scrolling, so using it as header input moves the entire title group once per hour.

**How to apply:** Keep Month/Year title widgets inside the same vertical scrollable as their calendar content, reserve the same title-slot height when the shell owns the title during transitions, and let the scroll viewport paint into the edge band. Derive any remaining shell-title translation from `pixels - minScrollExtent`, flooring at zero but leaving positive movement unbounded; normalize and clamp that value only when driving the edge effect. Filter notifications to scrollables that actually own the header, and use distinct scopes for calendar views and day submodes. Keep the glass surface color matched to the content background and leave landscape fills unchanged.
